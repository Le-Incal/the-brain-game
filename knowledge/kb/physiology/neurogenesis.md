# Neurogenesis

**Say it:** neurogenesis (NOOR-oh-JEN-eh-sis), progenitor (pro-JEN-ih-tor), dentate gyrus (DEN-tate JY-rus), subventricular (sub-ven-TRIK-yu-lar)

## In one breath

Neurogenesis means making new brain cells. Almost all the neurons in your thinking cortex were made before you were born, and your cerebellum kept adding tiny ones through your first year. Scientists are still working out whether grown-up brains make a few new ones.

## The tour

- **What it is:** The birth of new neurons from neural stem cells and their descendants.
- **How it functions:** Neural stem cells divide. Some copies stay as stem cells; others become progenitors that mature into a specific type of neuron or into glia (astrocytes and oligodendrocytes). New neurons then migrate to their final position and wire in.
- **What it's connected to:** Stem cell zones lining the fluid-filled ventricles, and in adults, small niches in the hippocampus.
- **What it controls:** It builds the brain before birth. In adult animals, new hippocampal neurons contribute to memory, spatial learning and mood regulation.

Memorable fact: averaged across pregnancy, the developing brain adds roughly 250,000 neurons every minute.

## Before birth

- The neural tube forms around weeks 3 to 4 of pregnancy. Its front end becomes the brain and the rest the spinal cord.
- Most cortical neurons are born in the first half of pregnancy, then migrate outward along radial glia to build the cortex layer by layer, inside out.
- The big exception is the cerebellum. Its granule cells, the most numerous neurons in the brain, are mostly generated after birth, during the first year of life.
- Failures of migration cause conditions such as lissencephaly (see anatomy/gyri_and_sulci.md).

## In adults: the honest state of the science

- **In rodents and many other mammals:** well established in two niches. The dentate gyrus of the hippocampus (subgranular zone) adds neurons that help with memory and mood. The subventricular zone sends new neurons to the olfactory bulb. Exercise increases it; stress, depression and ageing reduce it.
- **In humans:** debated, and the agent must say so.
  - Eriksson et al. 1998 gave the first evidence of new hippocampal neurons in adult humans.
  - Spalding et al. 2013 used carbon-14 from Cold War nuclear tests to estimate about 700 new neurons per day in each hippocampus.
  - Sorrells et al. 2018 found new neurons were rare by ages 7 to 13 and undetectable in adults.
  - Boldrini et al. 2018 found progenitors and immature neurons in healthy people aged 14 to 79.
  - Moreno-Jiménez et al. 2019 found thousands of immature neurons up to age 87, fewer in Alzheimer's, and argued that tissue handling explained earlier negative results.
  - Franjic et al. 2022 found clear neurogenic signatures in mice, pigs and monkeys but almost none in adult humans.
  - Dumitru et al. 2025 (Science, Frisén lab) found dividing neural progenitors in adult human hippocampus, with large differences between individuals.
- **Safe summary:** Adult humans probably make some new hippocampal neurons, at low and variable levels. What they do in humans is not yet known.
- **Human smell pathway:** The route to the olfactory bulb largely shuts down by about 18 months of age in humans.

## For the expert

- **Microglia are not born from neural stem cells.** They come from yolk-sac immune precursors.
- **History:** Joseph Altman reported adult neurogenesis in rats in the 1960s; Michael Kaplan confirmed it in 1977; Fernando Nottebohm showed seasonal neuron birth in songbirds in the 1980s. In 1992, Reynolds and Weiss, and separately Bartlett and Richards, isolated neural stem cells from adult mouse brain.
- **Therapeutic interest:** Stem cell and progenitor approaches for Parkinson's, stroke and spinal cord injury remain mostly experimental.

## Common questions

- **Can my brain grow new brain cells?** Maybe a few, in a memory area called the hippocampus. Scientists are still debating how many.
- **Does exercise grow new brain cells?** In animals, clearly. In people, exercise benefits the brain, but we cannot yet say new neurons are the reason.
- **So how do I learn if I don't get new neurons?** Mostly by strengthening and rewiring the connections between the neurons you already have.

## Careful with

- Never state human adult neurogenesis as confirmed or as disproven.
- Do not claim adult humans grow new amygdala neurons.
- Do not claim the human smell system is renewed by new neurons.
