# Types of Neurons

**Say it:** interneuron (IN-ter-noor-on), pseudounipolar (SOO-doh-yoo-nih-POH-lar), multipolar (mul-tee-POH-lar), pyramidal (pih-RAM-ih-dal)

## In one breath

There are three main jobs for neurons. Sensory neurons feel, motor neurons move, and interneurons connect the two and do the thinking in between.

## The tour

- **What they are:** Neurons are sorted by job, by shape, by the chemical they release, and by the genes they use.
- **How they function:**
  - **Sensory neurons** fire when something touches, heats, lights up or chemically activates their endings, then carry that news inward.
  - **Motor neurons** carry commands outward to muscles.
  - **Interneurons** (in the broad sense: any neuron that is neither sensory nor motor) link neurons to each other, forming the circuits that process information. In that broad sense they are by far the largest group in the brain.
- **What they're connected to:** Sensory neurons connect skin, eyes, ears and organs to the spinal cord and brain. Interneurons connect sensory to motor neurons and to each other. Motor neurons connect the spinal cord and brainstem to muscle.
- **What they control:** Sensation, movement, and everything between.

Memorable fact: touching a hot pan uses all three. A sensory neuron reports heat, an interneuron in the spinal cord passes it on, and a motor neuron pulls your hand away.

## For the expert

- **Upper vs lower motor neurons:** Upper motor neurons start in the motor cortex (region 5) and brainstem and descend to the spinal cord. Lower motor neurons run from spinal cord and brainstem to skeletal muscle. Upper damage gives stiffness and brisk reflexes; lower damage gives weakness, wasting and lost reflexes.
- **Autonomic motor neurons:** Smooth muscle, heart muscle and glands are driven by a two-neuron autonomic chain, not by somatic motor neurons. The gut also has its own enteric neurons.
- **Shapes:**
  - Pseudounipolar: one process that splits in two; touch and body-position neurons in the dorsal root ganglia.
  - Bipolar: one input and one output process; retina, smell, inner ear.
  - Multipolar: many dendrites, one axon; motor neurons and most brain neurons.
- **Projection neurons vs local interneurons:** Neuroscientists also use "interneuron" in a narrower sense: local circuit cells. Cortical pyramidal cells are projection neurons, sending long excitatory axons using glutamate, and make up about 80% of cortical neurons. Local interneurons (the other roughly 20%) are mostly inhibitory, using GABA, and shape timing and rhythm. Basket and chandelier cells are classic examples.
- **Signature neurons:** Betz cells (giant pyramidal cells of primary motor cortex, region 5); Purkinje cells (cerebellum, region 19), with a flat, fan-like dendritic tree and up to about 200,000 inputs each.
- **Classification today:** Single-cell gene sequencing atlases identify thousands of cell types across the brain. Classification is still evolving.

## On the model

- Region 5 Primary Motor: home of the upper motor neurons and Betz cells.
- Region 7 Primary Somatosensory: receives the relayed sensory stream.
- Region 19 Cerebellum: home of Purkinje cells.

## Common questions

- **How many kinds of neurons are there?** Three big jobs, but scientists now count thousands of specific types.
- **Which neurons let me move?** Motor neurons, commanded by the motor cortex.
- **What's the biggest neuron?** Among the longest are the motor neurons running from the spinal cord to the foot, and the touch neurons running from the toe all the way up to the brainstem. Both stretch over a metre.

## Careful with

- Not all sensory neurons are pseudounipolar. Vision, smell and hearing use bipolar neurons.
- Somatic motor neurons do not control stomach muscle. The autonomic and enteric systems do.
