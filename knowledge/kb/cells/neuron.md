# What Is a Neuron?

**Say it:** neuron (NOOR-on), dendrite (DEN-drite), soma (SOH-ma), axon (AX-on), synapse (SIN-aps)

## In one breath

A neuron is a brain cell that sends messages. It listens with its branches, decides whether to pass the message on, and then sends it down a long wire to the next cell.

## The tour

- **What it is:** The basic signalling cell of the brain and nervous system.
- **How it functions:** Think of a tree. The branches (dendrites) collect messages from other neurons. The trunk (cell body) adds them up. If the total is strong enough, the neuron fires an electrical pulse down its root-like cable (the axon). At the axon's tips, the pulse releases chemical messengers that cross a tiny gap (the synapse) to the next cell.
- **What it's connected to:** Other neurons, muscles and glands, through synapses. A typical cortical neuron connects with thousands of others.
- **What it controls:** Everything the nervous system does, from feeling a breeze to forming a thought, is the work of neurons signalling to one another.

Memorable fact: the human brain has about 86 billion neurons.

## Parts of a neuron

- **Dendrites:** Branches that receive input. Many are covered in tiny bumps called dendritic spines, about a micrometre across, where most excitatory synapses land. Spines change shape and number with learning.
- **Cell body (soma):** Holds the nucleus with the cell's DNA and makes most of its proteins.
- **Axon hillock and initial segment:** Where the axon leaves the cell body. This is the trigger zone where the electrical pulse usually starts.
- **Axon:** The output cable. See cells/axon.md.
- **Axon terminals:** Release neurotransmitter onto the next cell at the synapse.

## For the expert

- **Neuron count:** About 86 billion (Azevedo et al. 2009, isotropic fractionation, Herculano-Houzel lab). Roughly 69 billion are in the cerebellum, about 16 billion in the cerebral cortex.
- **Glia ratio:** Roughly 1:1 across the whole brain, varying by region. The old 10:1 figure is a myth.
- **Synapse locations:** Not only on dendrites. Axo-somatic (onto the cell body) and axo-axonic (onto the axon initial segment) synapses exist and are often strongly inhibitory.
- **Neuron doctrine:** Santiago Ramón y Cajal showed neurons are separate cells, not a continuous net, using Camillo Golgi's silver stain. They shared the 1906 Nobel Prize while disagreeing on this very point.
- **Exceptions:** Some neurons, such as retinal amacrine cells, lack a classic axon.
- **Longevity:** Most neurons are not replaced. Many cortical neurons last a whole lifetime.

## On the model

Neurons are microscopic. Every painted region is made of millions of them. The agent can use any region to say "this patch alone holds millions of my neurons."

## Common questions

- **How many neurons do I have?** About 86 billion.
- **How small is a neuron?** Most cell bodies are about a tenth to a fifth the width of a human hair, but some axons are over a metre long.
- **Do neurons touch each other?** Usually not quite. There is a tiny gap, the synapse, which chemical messengers cross.

## Careful with

- Do not say 100 billion neurons. Use about 86 billion.
- Do not say glia outnumber neurons ten to one.
