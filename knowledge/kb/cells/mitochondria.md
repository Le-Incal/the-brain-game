# Mitochondria

**Say it:** mitochondria (my-toh-KON-dree-a), mitochondrion (my-toh-KON-dree-on), cristae (KRIS-tee), ATP (A-T-P)

## In one breath

Mitochondria are tiny power stations inside your cells. They turn food and the air you breathe into energy, and brain cells need a lot of it.

## The tour

- **What they are:** Small structures inside nearly every cell that make usable energy. They were once free-living bacteria that moved into an early host cell well over a billion years ago and stayed.
- **How they function:** They use oxygen to break down fuel from food, especially glucose, and store the released energy in a molecule called ATP that powers the cell.
- **What they're connected to:** The cell's nucleus, which supplies most of the genes they need. In neurons, they are carried along axons to the synapses, where energy demand is highest.
- **What they control:** The energy supply for signalling. In neurons, most of that energy runs the pumps that reset the cell after each electrical pulse and recycles neurotransmitters at synapses.

Memorable fact: the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's energy at rest. Hard thinking barely changes that; the brain is always busy.

## For the expert

- **Structure:** Outer membrane, inner membrane folded into cristae, and the matrix. Oxidative phosphorylation happens on the inner membrane.
- **Own DNA:** A small circular genome of 37 genes (13 proteins, 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs), versus about 20,000 genes in nuclear DNA. Inherited almost always from the mother only.
- **Mutation rate:** Mitochondrial DNA mutates much faster than nuclear DNA, partly because it is copied often and sits near reactive oxygen production.
- **Neuronal roles:** Powering the Na⁺/K⁺ pump and synaptic transmission; buffering calcium; positioned at synapses and nodes of Ranvier by axonal transport.
- **Disease:** Mitochondrial disorders often hit brain and muscle first because they use the most energy. Effects can include seizures, stroke-like episodes, vision and hearing loss, and weakness. The first disease-causing mitochondrial DNA mutations were identified in 1988. Mitochondrial dysfunction is implicated in Parkinson's and other neurodegenerative diseases.
- **Origin:** An alphaproteobacterium engulfed by an archaeal-related host, roughly 1.5 to 2 billion years ago.

## On the model

Not visible. Present in every neuron and glial cell in every region.

## Common questions

- **Why does my brain need so much energy?** Neurons are constantly pumping ions to stay ready to fire, even when you're resting.
- **Do I burn more calories thinking hard?** Only slightly. Most of the brain's energy goes to background activity.
- **Where did mitochondria come from?** They were once bacteria that partnered with our ancestors' cells.

## Careful with

- The "free radicals cause ageing" idea is debated. Do not present it as settled.
- Mature human red blood cells have no mitochondria; almost every other cell does.
