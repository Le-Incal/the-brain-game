# Axons

**Say it:** axon (AX-on), myelin (MY-eh-lin), Ranvier (RAHN-vee-ay), saltatory (SAL-ta-tor-ee), collateral (ko-LAT-er-al)

## In one breath

An axon is the long tail of a neuron that carries its message away to other cells, like an electrical wire. Many are wrapped in a fatty coat that makes messages travel faster.

## The tour

- **What it is:** The single output fibre of a neuron, much thinner than a hair, carrying electrical impulses away from the cell body.
- **How it functions:** An electrical pulse travels to the axon's tips. There it triggers release of chemical messengers into the synapse, which start a new electrical signal in the next cell. A fatty wrap called myelin insulates many axons so signals move faster and farther.
- **What it's connected to:** At one end, its own cell body. At the other, other neurons, muscles or glands. Side branches (collaterals) let one neuron talk to many targets.
- **What it controls:** How far, how fast and to whom a neuron's message is delivered.

Memorable fact: some axons, running from the spinal cord to the toes, are over a metre long, from a cell body you could barely see.

## For the expert

- **Segments:** Axon hillock and axon initial segment (trigger zone, very high density of voltage-gated sodium channels); myelinated internodes; nodes of Ranvier (bare gaps rich in sodium channels); terminals.
- **Saltatory conduction:** The impulse jumps from node to node, increasing speed and saving energy.
- **Speed:** From about 0.5 m/s in thin unmyelinated fibres (slow, burning pain) to about 120 m/s in thick myelinated fibres (muscle position sense).
- **Diameter:** About 0.1 to 20 micrometres.
- **Myelin makers:** Oligodendrocytes in the CNS (one cell myelinates segments of up to about 50 axons); Schwann cells in the PNS (one cell per segment).
- **Axonal transport:** Motor proteins carry cargo along microtubules, out to the terminals (kinesin) and back to the cell body (dynein). Mitochondria ride this system to the synapses.
- **Disease:** Axon degeneration features in motor neurone disease (ALS), Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. In multiple sclerosis, the immune system attacks and strips away myelin. Microtubule-stabilising enzymes such as αTAT1 (studied as mec-17 in worms) help keep axons healthy.
- **Electrical synapses:** Some neurons connect through gap junctions that pass current directly, without chemical messengers.

## On the model

Invisible individually, but bundles of axons make up the white matter beneath the cortex and the brainstem's long tracts (region 20).

## Common questions

- **Why is white matter white?** Myelin is fatty, and fat looks pale.
- **How fast do nerve signals travel?** From slower than walking to faster than a racing car, depending on the axon's thickness and myelin.
- **Why does a stubbed toe hurt twice?** Fast myelinated fibres deliver the sharp first pain, and slow unmyelinated fibres bring the dull ache a moment later.

## Careful with

- Do not say all synapses are chemical.
- Do not say all axons are myelinated.
