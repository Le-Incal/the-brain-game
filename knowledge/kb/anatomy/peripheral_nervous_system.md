# Peripheral Nervous System

**Say it:** afferent (AF-er-ent), efferent (EF-er-ent), autonomic (aw-toh-NOM-ik), enteric (en-TER-ik), sciatic (sy-AT-ik)

## In one breath

The peripheral nervous system is all the nerves outside your brain and spinal cord. They are like wires running to your fingers, toes, heart and tummy.

## The tour

- **What it is:** Every nerve and cluster of nerve cells outside the brain and spinal cord.
- **How it functions:** Nerves are bundles of axons wrapped in connective tissue with their own blood vessels. Afferent (sensory) fibres carry information toward the central nervous system. Efferent (motor) fibres carry commands away from it to muscles, glands and organs.
- **What it's connected to:** The brain through 12 pairs of cranial nerves, the spinal cord through 31 pairs of spinal nerves, and on the other end every muscle, organ and sense receptor.
- **What it controls:** It delivers every movement command and carries every sensation. Its automatic branch runs heartbeat, breathing rate, digestion, sweating and pupil size.

Memorable fact: the sciatic nerve, from the lower back down the leg, is the longest and widest nerve in the body.

## For the expert

- **Somatic division:** Voluntary motor control of skeletal muscle, and sensation from skin, muscles and joints.
- **Autonomic division:**
  - Sympathetic ("fight or flight"): raises heart rate, widens pupils, diverts blood to muscles. Preganglionic neurons sit in the T1 to L2 cord.
  - Parasympathetic ("rest and digest"): slows the heart, drives digestion. Arises from the brainstem (cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X, with the vagus doing most of the work) and the sacral cord.
  - Both use a two-neuron chain: preganglionic to a ganglion, then postganglionic to the organ.
- **Enteric division:** A large mesh of neurons in the gut wall that can run digestion on its own. Some texts place it under autonomic; others treat it as a third division.
- **Myelin:** Schwann cells myelinate peripheral axons, one cell per segment of one axon.
- **Repair:** Peripheral nerves can regrow after injury far better than CNS axons, roughly a millimetre a day, guided by Schwann cells.
- **Vulnerability:** No bony shield, so nerves are easier to compress or injure.

## On the model

Peripheral nerves are not modelled. Cranial nerves would leave from the underside of the brain and brainstem. The agent can rotate to the inferior view and face region 20 to show roughly where most of them emerge.

## Common questions

- **Is the vagus nerve part of the brain?** It starts in the brainstem but is a peripheral nerve. It wanders down to the heart, lungs and gut, which is how it got its name, from the Latin for "wandering."
- **Why does my hand move off a hot stove before I feel the heat?** Sensory fibres reach the spinal cord, which triggers the withdrawal reflex. The message to the brain, where you feel it, arrives a moment later.
- **Can nerves heal?** Peripheral nerves often can, slowly. Nerve fibres in the brain and spinal cord rarely regrow.

## Careful with

- The optic nerve is technically part of the central nervous system, despite its name.
- The sciatic nerve reaches the foot through its branches, the tibial and common fibular nerves.
