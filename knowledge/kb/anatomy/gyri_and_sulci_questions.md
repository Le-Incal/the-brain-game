# Questions About Gyri and Sulci

Questions are written the way players ask them. Answers are short enough to speak. Level tags: [child] [curious] [expert].

## The basics

**Why is the brain wrinkly?** [child]
The brain has a lot of thinking material, more than would fit in your head if it were flat. Folding it up lets it all squeeze inside, like scrunching a big blanket into a small bag.

**What are the bumps called? And the lines?** [child]
The bumps are gyri, and one bump is a gyrus. The grooves are sulci, and one groove is a sulcus. A really deep groove is called a fissure.

**Is a sulcus the same as a fissure?** [curious]
A fissure is just a very deep sulcus. The two biggest are the longitudinal fissure, which splits the left and right halves, and the lateral fissure, which runs along the side above the temporal lobe.

**How much brain is hidden in the grooves?** [curious]
About two-thirds of the cortex is tucked inside the sulci. What you see on the surface is only the top third.

**How big would the brain be if you ironed it flat?** [curious]
About 2,200 square centimetres, roughly three and a half sheets of printer paper, but only about two and a half millimetres thick on average.

## Landmarks on the tour

**Where does movement end and touch begin?** [curious]
At the central sulcus. The ridge in front of it, the precentral gyrus, sends movement commands. The ridge behind it, the postcentral gyrus, feels touch. Action → face region 5, then 7.

**Where is the part that sees?** [curious]
At the back, along a groove on the inner surface called the calcarine sulcus. The primary visual cortex lines its banks. Action → face region 17.

**Where is hearing?** [curious]
Mostly hidden. The primary auditory cortex sits on small ridges called Heschl's gyri, buried inside the lateral fissure. The model shades the outer temporal surface instead, so you have something to aim at. Action → face region 11 and say it is buried.

**What's the curved fold on the inside of the brain?** [curious]
The cingulate gyrus. It arches over the corpus callosum, the bridge between the hemispheres. Action → face region 16.

## Going deeper

**Do smarter people have more wrinkles?** [curious]
No. Within humans, folding is at best weakly linked to intelligence and cannot tell you how smart someone is. Across animals, bigger brains tend to fold more, whether or not the animal is especially clever.

**Do all animals have wrinkly brains?** [child]
No. Mice and rats have smooth brains. Cats, dogs, dolphins and people have folded ones.

**Is everyone's fold pattern the same?** [curious]
The big landmarks are the same in nearly everyone. The smaller folds vary, like fingerprints. Even identical twins differ.

**When does the brain get its folds?** [curious]
Before birth. A baby's brain starts smooth. The first grooves appear around the third month of pregnancy, and most of the main ones form between about weeks 16 and 30.

**Why does the cortex fold at all, mechanically?** [expert]
There is no single accepted answer. Leading ideas are uneven growth between the upper and lower layers of cortex, pulling forces from white matter fibres, and a large population of outer radial glia that expands the cortex sideways faster than the layers beneath it. The skull alone does not explain it.

**What happens if the brain doesn't fold?** [expert]
That condition is lissencephaly, meaning smooth brain. It comes from neurons failing to migrate into place during development, often due to mutations in genes like LIS1 or DCX that affect the cell's internal skeleton. It is rare (the isolated form affects about 1 in 100,000 births) and usually causes seizures and developmental delay.

**What's the difference between a limiting and an axial sulcus?** [expert]
A limiting sulcus sits on a border between areas with different jobs, like the central sulcus between motor and sensory cortex. An axial sulcus runs along the middle of a fast-growing area, like the calcarine sulcus through primary visual cortex.

**Are the folds why the hatching follows curves on this model?** [curious]
Yes. The engraving lines follow the real surface of the model, so they bend over every gyrus and dive into every sulcus.

## Safety and tone

If a player asks whether a smooth brain or "fewer wrinkles" means someone is less intelligent, correct it kindly. Never use "smooth brain" as an insult, even jokingly.
