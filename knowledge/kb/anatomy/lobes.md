# Lobes of the Brain

**Say it:** frontal (FRUN-tal), parietal (pa-RYE-eh-tal), temporal (TEM-por-al), occipital (ok-SIP-ih-tal), insula (IN-syu-la)

## In one breath

Each half of the brain has four big sections called lobes. The front one helps you plan, the top one feels touch, the side one hears, and the back one sees.

## The tour

- **What they are:** The cerebral cortex of each hemisphere is divided into four lobes: frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital. Deep grooves mark most of the borders.
- **How they function:** Each lobe holds a primary area that receives raw input or sends raw output, surrounded by association areas that make sense of it and combine it with other information.
- **What they're connected to:** Each other, through short fibres under the cortex; the opposite hemisphere, through the corpus callosum; and deep structures like the thalamus, basal ganglia and hippocampus.
- **What they control:**
  - **Frontal:** planning, reasoning, decisions, personality, emotional regulation, voluntary movement, and speech production.
  - **Parietal:** body sensation (touch, temperature, pressure, pain), knowing where your body is, spatial attention, and guiding your hands toward things.
  - **Temporal:** hearing, understanding language, recognising faces and objects, and, on its inner side, forming memories.
  - **Occipital:** vision, from first detection of edges to working out depth, motion and what an object is.

Memorable fact: the lobes are named after the skull bones that cover them.

## For the expert

- **Borders:** The central sulcus separates frontal from parietal. The lateral (Sylvian) fissure separates the temporal lobe from both frontal and parietal. The parieto-occipital sulcus, seen clearly only on the medial surface, separates parietal from occipital.
- **Primary areas:** Primary motor cortex on the precentral gyrus (Brodmann 4); primary somatosensory cortex on the postcentral gyrus (3, 1, 2); primary auditory cortex on the transverse temporal (Heschl's) gyri (41, 42); primary visual cortex along the calcarine sulcus (17).
- **Other lobes:** Many texts count the insula, buried inside the lateral fissure, as a fifth lobe. Some count a limbic lobe on the medial surface (cingulate and parahippocampal gyri).
- **Visual streams:** The ventral "what" stream runs from occipital into temporal cortex (object and face recognition). The dorsal "where/how" stream runs into parietal cortex (location and action).
- **Lateralisation:** Language is left-dominant in most people (about 95% of right-handers, roughly 70% of left-handers). This is a specific finding, not evidence for "left-brain logic, right-brain creativity."

## Common questions

- **Which lobe is the biggest?** The frontal lobe, about a third of the cortex.
- **Do the lobes work alone?** No. Almost every task, like reading this sentence aloud, uses several lobes at once.
- **Are both sides the same?** Nearly, in shape. Some jobs lean to one side, like language to the left in most people.

## Careful with

- Do not reduce the parietal lobe to touch, or the frontal lobe to "thinking." Both do far more.
- Never say "left brain is logical, right brain is creative." That is a myth.
