# Central Nervous System

**Say it:** meninges (meh-NIN-jeez), cerebrospinal (seh-REE-bro-SPY-nal)

## In one breath

The brain and the spinal cord together are the body's control centre. Messages from your whole body travel in, and instructions travel back out.

## The tour

- **What it is:** Two connected structures, the brain inside the skull and the spinal cord inside the backbone. Everything outside them is the peripheral nervous system.
- **How it functions:** It takes in signals from the senses and the organs, decides what they mean, and sends commands to muscles and glands. Some fast decisions, like pulling a hand from a hot stove, are made in the spinal cord before the brain catches up.
- **What it's connected to:** The rest of the body, through 12 pairs of cranial nerves from the brain and 31 pairs of spinal nerves from the cord.
- **What it controls:** Movement, sensation, reflexes, breathing and heartbeat, and every thought, feeling and memory.

Memorable fact: the brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's energy at rest.

## For the expert

- **Grey and white matter:** Grey matter holds neuron cell bodies, dendrites, glia and dense capillaries. White matter is mostly myelinated axons, which conduct faster and more reliably. The arrangement flips: in the cerebrum grey matter is on the outside (cortex) with white matter beneath; in the spinal cord white matter is outside and grey matter forms a butterfly-shaped core.
- **Protection:** Three layers. Bone (skull, vertebrae); three meninges (dura mater, arachnoid mater, pia mater); and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), which cushions the brain and lets it float, reducing its effective weight.
- **Developmental divisions:** Forebrain (cerebrum plus diencephalon: thalamus, hypothalamus, pineal gland), midbrain, and hindbrain (pons, medulla oblongata, cerebellum). The midbrain, pons and medulla together form the brainstem.
- **Scale:** About 86 billion neurons, with roughly as many glia.
- **Edge cases:** The retina and optic nerve are developmentally part of the CNS, not the peripheral system.

## On the model

The brain and the top of the brainstem are visible. The spinal cord is not part of the model; the brainstem ends where the cord would begin. Meninges and CSF are not modelled, so the model appears as the brain does once they are removed.

## Common questions

- **Is the spinal cord part of the brain?** No, but it is part of the central nervous system. It is the brain's main cable to the body and does some processing of its own.
- **Why is the brain wrinkly and pink in pictures but grey here?** Living grey matter is pinkish because of its blood supply. Preserved specimens turn grey.
- **What protects the brain?** Bone, three membranes, and a fluid cushion.

## Careful with

- Some textbooks and websites still say 100 billion neurons. The current estimate is about 86 billion.
- Do not say grey matter is "the thinking part" and white matter is "just wiring." White matter tracts determine how fast and how well regions work together.
