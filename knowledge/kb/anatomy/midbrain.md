# The Midbrain

**Say it:** mesencephalon (mez-en-SEF-a-lon), colliculus (ko-LIK-yu-lus), colliculi (ko-LIK-yu-lye), tegmentum (teg-MEN-tum), substantia nigra (sub-STAN-shee-a NY-gra), periaqueductal (pair-ee-AK-weh-DUK-tal)

## In one breath

The midbrain is a small piece at the top of the brainstem. It helps your eyes move, turns your head toward a sudden sound, and makes a chemical called dopamine that helps you move smoothly.

## The tour

- **What it is:** The shortest of the three brainstem parts, sitting between the forebrain above and the pons below.
- **How it functions:** It relays signals between the brain above and the body below, makes fast reflex turns toward sights and sounds, and produces dopamine.
- **What it's connected to:** The forebrain through the cerebral peduncles; the thalamus; the pons, medulla and spinal cord below; and two cranial nerves, III (oculomotor) and IV (trochlear).
- **What it controls:** Most eye movements and lifting the eyelid, pupil size, orienting toward sudden sights and sounds, smooth movement through dopamine, alertness, and pain suppression.

Memorable fact: the substantia nigra, "black substance," really is dark. Its dopamine neurons contain a pigment called neuromelanin.

## Key parts

- **Tectum (roof):** Two pairs of bumps called colliculi.
  - **Superior colliculi:** steer the eyes and head toward something seen.
  - **Inferior colliculi:** relay hearing up to the thalamus and help locate where a sound comes from.
- **Tegmentum (floor):**
  - **Red nucleus:** part of motor circuits linking cerebellum and cortex. Iron-rich, slightly pink in fresh tissue.
  - **Periaqueductal grey:** surrounds the fluid channel; key centre for suppressing pain and for defensive responses.
  - **Ventral tegmental area (VTA):** a dopamine source for reward and motivation.
- **Substantia nigra:** dopamine neurons that feed the basal ganglia. Their loss causes Parkinson's disease.
- **Cerebral peduncles:** large columns of descending fibres from the cortex to the brainstem and spinal cord.
- **Cerebral aqueduct:** the narrow canal carrying cerebrospinal fluid through the midbrain.

## For the expert

- The superior colliculus drives saccades and orienting. Conscious vision travels a different route: retina to lateral geniculate nucleus of the thalamus to V1. The colliculus is not "where we see."
- Nerve III controls four of six eye muscles plus the eyelid lifter and the pupil constrictor. Nerve IV (trochlear) is the only cranial nerve to exit from the back of the brainstem, and the only one that crosses before it exits.
- Two dopamine systems: nigrostriatal (movement, Parkinson's) and mesolimbic/mesocortical from the VTA (reward, motivation, addiction).
- Narrowing or blockage of the cerebral aqueduct is a classic cause of hydrocephalus.

## On the model

The midbrain is largely hidden between the hemispheres and the cerebellum. Only a sliver may show from below. It has no paint of its own: it is the top of region 20, the Brain Stem, which covers the midbrain, pons and medulla. The agent faces region 20 and says the midbrain is its uppermost part.

## Common questions

- **What does the midbrain do?** It moves the eyes, snaps your attention toward surprises, and makes dopamine.
- **What part of the brain causes Parkinson's?** A loss of dopamine neurons in the substantia nigra in the midbrain.
- **Why do I flinch toward a loud bang?** Your inferior and superior colliculi turn your eyes and head before you consciously decide.

## Careful with

- Do not say the midbrain "processes vision before the occipital lobe." It handles orienting, not seeing.
- The pons is not the bridge between hemispheres; that is the corpus callosum.
