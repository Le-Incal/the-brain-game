# The Forebrain

**Say it:** cerebrum (seh-REE-brum), diencephalon (dy-en-SEF-a-lon), thalamus (THAL-a-mus), hypothalamus (hy-po-THAL-a-mus), pineal (PIN-ee-al), basal ganglia (BAY-sal GANG-glee-a)

## In one breath

The forebrain is the biggest part of the brain. It is where you think, talk, remember, feel and decide what to do.

## The tour

- **What it is:** The largest of the three developmental divisions. It includes the cerebrum (both hemispheres, with the cortex and the structures beneath it) and the diencephalon (thalamus, hypothalamus, pineal gland).
- **How it functions:** The cortex does higher processing. Deep structures relay signals, select actions, and keep the body in balance.
- **What it's connected to:** The other hemisphere through the corpus callosum; the brainstem and spinal cord through descending and ascending tracts; the hormone system through the hypothalamus and pituitary gland.
- **What it controls:** Thought, language, planning, all conscious senses, voluntary movement, memory, emotion, sleep timing, body temperature, hunger and thirst.

Memorable fact: the forebrain makes up about 85% of the brain's weight.

## Key parts

- **Cerebral cortex:** The folded outer layer, divided into lobes. See anatomy/lobes.md.
- **Basal ganglia:** Deep nuclei (caudate, putamen, globus pallidus, with the subthalamic nucleus and substantia nigra as partners). They help select which movements and habits go ahead and which are held back. Damage to their dopamine input causes Parkinson's disease.
- **Thalamus:** A paired egg-shaped mass at the centre. It relays every sense except smell to the cortex, and sits in loops with the basal ganglia and cerebellum that shape movement. It takes part in attention, arousal and sleep states.
- **Hypothalamus:** About the size of an almond, below the thalamus. It controls body temperature, hunger, thirst, the sleep-wake clock, and hormones through the pituitary gland.
- **Pineal gland:** About the size of a grain of rice and shaped like a pinecone. It releases melatonin at night.
- **Also forebrain:** the hippocampus, amygdala and olfactory bulbs.

## For the expert

- **Grey vs white matter:** In adults the cerebral hemispheres are a little over half grey matter and a little under half white matter by volume.
- **Embryology:** Derived from the prosencephalon, which divides into telencephalon (cerebrum) and diencephalon.
- **Thalamic relays:** Lateral geniculate nucleus (vision), medial geniculate nucleus (hearing), ventral posterior nuclei (touch and pain), ventral lateral and ventral anterior nuclei (motor loops).
- **Circadian control:** The suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus sets the body clock and signals the pineal gland.
- **Basal ganglia loops:** Direct ("go") and indirect ("no-go") pathways, modulated by dopamine from the substantia nigra pars compacta.

## On the model

The cortex is the visible surface, painted as regions 1 to 18. The thalamus, hypothalamus, basal ganglia and pineal gland are hidden inside and cannot be faced. The agent should say "deep inside me, beneath where you're looking."

## Common questions

- **What part of the brain is the biggest?** The forebrain, and within it the cerebrum.
- **What does the thalamus do?** It is a relay station. Almost every sense passes through it on the way to the cortex.
- **Where is the body's clock?** In the hypothalamus, which tells the pineal gland when to release melatonin.

## Careful with

- Do not say the thalamus "controls sleep." It participates in sleep and arousal alongside the brainstem and hypothalamus.
- Smell is the one sense that reaches the cortex without passing through the thalamus first.
