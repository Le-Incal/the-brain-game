# The Limbic System

**Say it:** limbic (LIM-bik), hippocampus (hip-oh-KAM-pus), amygdala (a-MIG-da-la), fornix (FOR-nix), mammillary (MAM-ih-lair-ee), cingulate (SING-gyu-lat)

## In one breath

The limbic system is a group of parts deep inside the brain that help you feel emotions, remember what happened, and stay safe.

## The tour

- **What it is:** A set of connected structures curving around the inside edge of each hemisphere, beneath the cortex and above the brainstem. "Limbic" means "border."
- **How it functions:** The hippocampus turns experiences into lasting memories and helps you find your way. The amygdala detects threats and attaches emotional weight to events. The cingulate cortex helps notice conflict and errors and links feeling to action. The hypothalamus turns emotion into body changes, like a racing heart.
- **What it's connected to:** The prefrontal and orbitofrontal cortex (which regulate emotion), the thalamus, the hypothalamus, the olfactory system, the brainstem, and the reward circuits of the basal ganglia.
- **What it controls:** Emotional responses, fear learning, forming new memories of events and facts, spatial navigation, motivation, and the body side of emotion: heart rate, stress hormones, appetite.

Memorable fact: "hippocampus" is Greek for seahorse, and "amygdala" is Greek for almond, both named for their shapes.

## Key parts

- **Hippocampus:** Paired, on the inner temporal lobe. Needed to form new memories of events and facts. For many memories, long-term storage gradually shifts to the cortex, though vivid personal memories may keep relying on the hippocampus. Contains place cells; its neighbour the entorhinal cortex contains grid cells, together forming a navigation system (Nobel Prize 2014).
- **Amygdala:** Paired, just in front of the hippocampus. Rapid threat detection and fear learning; also responds to reward and social signals. A fear memory can form after a single bad experience.
- **Cingulate gyrus:** Arches over the corpus callosum. Anterior part: attention, error and conflict monitoring, pain unpleasantness, emotion. Posterior part: memory and self-reflection, part of the default mode network.
- **Also included:** fornix (main output cable of the hippocampus), mammillary bodies, parahippocampal gyrus, septal nuclei, hypothalamus.

## For the expert

- **Patient H.M.:** Removal of both medial temporal lobes in 1953 left him unable to form new declarative memories while procedural learning remained (Scoville and Milner 1957).
- **Papez circuit:** hippocampus to fornix to mammillary bodies to anterior thalamus to cingulate gyrus to parahippocampal gyrus and back.
- **Concept critique:** The "limbic system" as a single emotion system is dated (LeDoux). Modern view: overlapping circuits, with the hippocampus mainly a memory structure and emotion distributed across amygdala, insula, prefrontal cortex and brainstem.
- **Adult neurogenesis** in the human hippocampus is debated. See physiology/neurogenesis.md. Do not state it as settled.

## On the model

Region 16 Cingulate Cortex is the only painted limbic region, visible on the medial surface and from below. The hippocampus and amygdala are hidden inside the temporal lobes. When asked, the agent can face region 12 or 14 and say "my hippocampus lies deep inside here, curled along the inner wall of my temporal lobe."

## Common questions

- **Where are memories stored?** The hippocampus helps form them, and over time many get spread across the cortex.
- **What part of the brain makes me scared?** The amygdala raises the alarm, but fear involves many regions working together.
- **Is the limbic system the "emotional brain"?** That is a useful starting picture, but emotion uses circuits all over the brain, including the frontal lobes.

## Careful with

- Basal ganglia are not core limbic structures; only the ventral striatum (nucleus accumbens) is limbic-linked.
- Do not claim adults definitely grow new amygdala or hippocampal neurons.
