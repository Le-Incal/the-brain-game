# The Spinal Cord

**Say it:** vertebrae (VER-teh-bray), cauda equina (KAW-da eh-KWY-na), conus medullaris (KOH-nus med-yu-LAR-is)

## In one breath

The spinal cord is a soft cable of nerves running down the inside of your backbone. It carries messages between your brain and your body, and it can make some quick moves on its own.

## The tour

- **What it is:** A column of nerve tissue that continues down from the brainstem through the spinal canal of the vertebrae.
- **How it functions:** It carries movement commands down from the brain to the muscles and carries sensations up from the body to the brain. It also runs reflexes by itself, like the knee-jerk or snatching your hand from something hot.
- **What it's connected to:** The brainstem above (it continues from the medulla), and 31 pairs of spinal nerves that branch out to the body.
- **What it controls:** It relays all voluntary movement below the neck, carries touch, pain, temperature and body position upward, and runs reflexes and some automatic body functions.

Memorable fact: the cord is only about a centimetre wide, roughly the width of a finger, and weighs about 35 grams.

## For the expert

- **Length and termination:** About 45 cm in adults. It does not run the full length of the spine. It ends around the L1 to L2 vertebrae at the conus medullaris; below that, nerve roots continue as the cauda equina. This is why lumbar punctures are done lower down.
- **Spinal nerves:** 31 pairs: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal. There are 8 cervical nerves but only 7 cervical vertebrae, because the first nerve exits above C1.
- **Internal layout:** White matter outside, grey matter in a butterfly-shaped core. Ventral (front) horns hold lower motor neurons. Dorsal (back) horns receive sensory input. Lateral horns (T1 to L2) hold sympathetic autonomic neurons.
- **Tracts and crossing:** The corticospinal (motor) tract mostly crosses in the lower medulla, so the left motor cortex drives the right side of the body. Fine touch and position (dorsal columns) cross in the medulla. Pain and temperature (spinothalamic tract) cross within a segment or two of entering the cord. This difference explains the split symptoms of a half-cord injury (Brown-Séquard syndrome).
- **Protection:** Vertebrae and discs, the three meninges, and CSF.
- **Reflexes:** The reflex arc completes in the cord, but the brain still receives the signal and can dampen or boost reflexes.

## On the model

The spinal cord is not modelled. The model ends at the brainstem (region 20), where the cord would attach. The agent can face region 20 and say "my spinal cord would continue from here."

## Common questions

- **Does the spinal cord go all the way down my back?** Not quite. In adults it ends in the lower back, around the bottom of the ribs. Below that, a bundle of nerve roots continues.
- **Can the spinal cord think?** It cannot think, but it can run reflexes and simple rhythmic patterns, like the stepping rhythm of walking, without the brain directing each step.
- **Why does a back injury cause paralysis?** If the cord is damaged, messages between the brain and the body below the injury cannot get through.

## Careful with

- Do not say the cord fills the whole spine.
- Do not say reflexes "skip" the brain completely. The brain still hears about them.
